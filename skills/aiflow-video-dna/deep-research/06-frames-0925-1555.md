## Method and evidence

- I read all 391 frames at 1 fps (`frames_1fps/s0565…s0955`).
- I pulled extra 10 fps contact sheets into `tmp/a6/` for every moment where the picture changed: 648–656, 690–696, 850–862, 893–901, 931–946. I also took full-resolution PNGs at 649.5, 650.8, 653.8, 669, 694, 807 and 941 s to sample colours.
- I took hard cuts from `cuts.csv` and every `scdet` score ≥ 4 (59 events in this range).
- **Zoom test:** I compared the browser bookmark-bar strip in all 391 frames against frame 700. Only 9 frames differ: 649–651 (subscribe overlay) and 939–944 (full-screen clip). The UI never changes scale.
- **Audio:** I computed 50 ms RMS on `audio16k.wav` and searched it for loud non-speech events.
- **Narration:** mapped from `transcript_translate.txt/.json` and checked against the original Hindi/Urdu `transcript_transcribe.json`.
- **Heatmap:** read from `heatmap.json` (100 buckets of 13.7 s each). I correlated it with how much the picture changes per second.
- **Comments:** keyword search of `comments.json`, 133 comments.

## 0. TL;DR — the strongest findings

1. **No camera work at all.** There are 0 zooms, 0 punch-ins and 0 pans in 390 s; every frame passed the bookmark-bar strip test. The edit is a raw 1:1 browser recording (1920×1032 inside 1080, with 24 px black bars top and bottom). The presenter's own mouse supplies all the emphasis: native text selection, scrolling and tab switching.
2. **The editor only adds 5 kinds of things:**
   - one animated subscribe card (3.4 s)
   - one meme overlay that hides a jump cut (1.6 s)
   - two speed-ramps over AI waits (≈6 s each)
   - one jump-cut montage (14 cuts in 18.7 s, 1.34 s per shot)
   - one full-screen playback of the AI output (5.6 s, black matte)

   Everything else is uncut screen recording driven by the voice-over.
3. **Music fills narration gaps.** Whenever the voice stops for 2.5 s or more over a sped-up or montage segment, loud non-speech audio (most likely music) jumps to peaks of −2.8 to −5 dBFS. Narration sits around −23 dBFS RMS, so the jump is about +18–20 dB. This happens at 693.8, 903.5–911 and 933.7–938.9 s.
4. **Prompts are shown, not given.** Every prompt is on screen at native UI size: 1.2–1.5 % of frame height, i.e. 13–16 px on a 1080 frame. Each is visible for 3–25 s, mostly inside scrolling 10-line text boxes. 29 of 133 comments (22 %) ask "where is the PDF / prompts". Viewers pause and rewind to copy.
5. **Rewatch tracks value, not motion.** The correlation between visual change per bucket and heatmap value is **r = −0.05**.
   - **Global peak (686–700 s, 1.00):** the clean script is finished, plus a speed-ramp that goes by too fast to follow, plus the jump into the "Voice" chapter, which teaches the free text-to-speech (TTS) tool.
   - **Second peak (919–947 s, 0.70–0.85):** a fast Grok "Animate Image" walkthrough that ends in a full-screen talking-character clip.
   - **Lowest values in the range** are two static waits: 755–768 s (0.20, a 17-s TTS spinner) and 659–672 s (0.21, a 17-s static script read).

## 6. Why it works

- **Utility in every frame.** The range is a complete production line in 6.5 minutes: 7 tools and 41 micro-steps, about one step every 9.5 s. Every step ends in a visible artifact (script, audio file 10:55, 29 prompts, images, talking clip). Viewers come for a recipe, and the edit never hides the recipe behind B-roll.
- **Raw UI equals credibility.** No zooms, no graphics and a real cursor read as "this really works, I'm not faking it". The audience is beginners who want to replicate click for click, and the 1:1 recording preserves exact UI positions.
- **Friction drives engagement.**
  - Prompts are visible but too small and too fast, so viewers pause, rewind and comment asking for the PDF (29 comments ≈ 22 % of all).
  - Comments mention a WhatsApp channel for the PDF, so the prompts also double as a funnel.
  - The heatmap concentrates exactly where the prompts and settings are.
- **Waits are monetised, not shown.** The two longest AI waits get the CTA (graphic plus verbal), a joke, a speed-ramp or a cut. The one wait left in at full length (TTS, 17 s) is the range's lowest-replay bucket, which shows the rule is right.
- **Music as a pace signal.** When the voice stops, loud rhythmic music marks "fast-forward", so silence never happens.
- **Payoff placed late in the range.** The first moving or speaking output appears at 15:39, near the end of the build section. It works as a reward and keeps the "how do I edit this?" question open for the Editing chapter.
- **Chapters line up with intent.** The Voice chapter starts 2 s before the TTS tool opens, which makes the most-wanted step one click away (global heatmap maximum).

## 7. Open questions and low-confidence items

- **Speed-ramp vs cuts.** 690–696 and 934–938 contain no hard cuts (scdet < 4), so the compression is most likely a speed ramp. The factors (≈4–6× and ≈5–10×) are estimated from UI progress, not measured.
- **Music bed.** The loud non-speech bursts are inferred to be music from their rhythm (≈0.6 s bursts every 0.9–1.4 s). I could not listen. Whether a faint bed sits under all narration (floor −40 to −47 dBFS) is unverified.
- **Streaming speed.** Whether ChatGPT's 29-scene stream (828–851) was sped up is unknown.
- **YouTube info card.** At about 15:00 the narrator says to watch another video on bulk generation. No card is visible in the frames; a YouTube i-card may have been added on the platform side.
- **Heatmap resolution.** Buckets are 13.7 s wide. Within 686–700 I cannot separate chapter-jump seeking from rewinds of the speed-ramp.
- **Blur coverage.** The blur box timing (≈700–785) was read at 1 fps; exact in/out frames were not measured.
- **Card dimensions.** The subscribe card's exact height (19 % vs 25 % H) depends on the animation phase sampled.

## Rules to copy

1. **Record the build section as raw 1:1 browser capture.** Use 1920×1032 inside a 1080 frame with 24 px black bars. Use **0 zooms or punch-ins** during tool walkthroughs (the source had 0 in 390 s). Let the cursor, text selection and scrolling carry the emphasis.
2. **One micro-step every 8–11 s.** Cover a full tool chain at about 40 steps per 6.5 minutes, switch app or tab every 12–21 s outside montages, and show 5–7 tools per 6.5 minutes.
3. **Use the 4-beat loop for every tool.**
   - Show the red Docs header of the prompt source (≥ 2 s).
   - Paste into the AI tool.
   - Select the placeholder or knob and hold it **4–10 s**.
   - Run, then reveal the result.
4. **Highlight with native text selection, never with drawn boxes or arrows.** Select the exact words within 0–2 s of naming them, and do it about every 14 s (≈27 times per 390 s).
5. **Fill the first long AI wait in a section with a graphic CTA.** Hard-cut in a 3.4 s animated subscribe card:
   - background dimmed to about 20 % brightness;
   - white card 38 % W × 19–25 % H, centred;
   - red #E80000 "SUBSCRIBE" → grey #DFDFDF "SUBSCRIBED" with a particle burst;
   - a hand-cursor click at about 1.6 s;
   - slide-down exit.

   Sync it to "subscribe while it generates". Allow at most **1 graphic CTA + 1 verbal CTA** per 6.5 minutes, each over a wait.
6. **Hide the end of an AI wait under a 1.5–2 s comedic overlay**, then jump-cut to the finished result underneath. Use an **original, harmless meme graphic** (for example our own character's shocked face or a cartoon pleading pose). Never use weapons.
7. **Never hold a static generating or reading screen longer than 8 s.** The source's 17-s holds were the range minimum (heatmap 0.20–0.21). For longer waits, speed-ramp at 4–6× or cut.
8. **When narration pauses for 2.5 s or more over a ramp or montage, open the music.** Peaks of about −5 dBFS are roughly +18 dB over the narration level of −23 dBFS RMS. Duck it back under the next spoken line within 0.3 s.
9. **Show the first 2–3 iterations of a repetitive task in full, then a jump-cut montage.** Use 1.2–1.5 s per shot (14 shots in 18.7 s), alternating source copy and target paste.
10. **Reveal video outputs full-screen once.** Hard-cut to the clip scaled to about 78 % W × 77 % H on pure black, play 5–6 s with its own audio, then hard-cut back to the tool page.
11. **Voice demo grammar.**
    - Show mode, style instruction and voice descriptor list for ≥ 5 s each.
    - Show the native player with the total length visible.
    - Play **5 s** of the generated voice at +3–4 dB over the narration.
    - No waveform graphic is needed.
12. **Keep copyable text at native UI size (1.2–1.5 % H), but put every prompt in a downloadable pack linked in the pinned comment and description.** The source's missing link produced 29 "where is the PDF" comments. Copy the on-screen behaviour; fix the delivery.
13. **Place each chapter marker 0–3 s before the step viewers want most.** The Voice chapter at −2 s produced the global replay maximum. Use **no** visual chapter cards; announce chapter changes verbally in one sentence.
14. **Narrate at about 190–200 words/min with ≥ 90 % voice coverage.** The screen action must follow the spoken instruction within 0–2 s. Put jokes, caveats and CTAs only over waits or static screens.
15. **Show a reference example clip for the target style (15–20 s) before the image step.** In our production use our own previous video or licensed footage, never another creator's video.
16. **Jump-cut away regenerations, retries and error dialogs.** Show any unavoidable pop-up for 2 s or less.
17. **End each tool chapter with a 3–5 s gallery scroll of all finished assets** as proof of volume.
18. **Use an ORIGINAL recurring character** in all image and video prompts. Give it its own name, silhouette, hair, clothing colour and one signature prop, in flat 2D vector style with thick black outlines and a white background. Lock it with a reference ("subject") image. Never prompt for or imitate a known TV or cartoon character.
19. **Mask privacy permanently with a soft-blur rectangle** over account areas (about 13 % W × 4 % H at the bottom left in this layout). Before export, check file pickers, downloads panels and bookmark bars, which the source leaked at 930–931 and 886–887.
20. **Use a click visualiser.** A thin ring about 3.7 % of frame height, pink-red (#E08A8A), visible 0.1–0.3 s per click. Keep the system cursor visible at all times.
21. **No full-screen text cards, lower thirds, arrows or editor-made split screens in the tool-walkthrough section.** The source used 0 of each in 390 s. The UI is the graphic.
22. **Put the first moving or speaking output near the end of the build section.** Hold it back until about 90 % of the section, cut to it full-screen, and pair it with the loudest audio moment (about −3 dBFS peak).
