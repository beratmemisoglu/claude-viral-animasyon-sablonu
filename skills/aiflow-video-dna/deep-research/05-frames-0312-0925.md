- **Zoom curve.** I measured scale numerically. At 10 fps I took the bounding-box width of the "What can I help with?" heading in each frame and divided it by the width in the un-zoomed frame. Results are below.
- **Cuts.** I combined `cuts.csv` (22 events in range) with `scdet_scores.csv` (every score ≥ 3). I also looked at frames, because tab switches between near-white pages score low.
- **Audio.**
  - 0.1 s and 0.05 s RMS on `audio16k.wav`.
  - Spectral centroid, flatness and FFT peaks on every loud non-speech event.
  - A pause-length histogram.
- **Narration.** I mapped `transcript_translate.json` (117 segments in range) onto the screen. Word counts come from both the translation and the original `transcript_transcribe.json`.

- **Comments.** Keyword search of all 133 comments.

## 6. Why it works

1. **Credibility through unedited reality.** Real searches, real vidIQ scores, real downloads and real AI waits make every step feel reproducible. The 1:1 light-mode browser with no graphics reads as "I'm literally doing it in front of you". This suits a tutorial audience that wants to copy, not be entertained. Evidence: comments ask for the prompt file, not for clarification.
2. **The prompt is the product.** The longest attention (heatmap 0.449) goes to the pattern formula. Withholding the PDF link turns prompt screens into freeze-frame moments, so replays cluster there.
3. **Low-cost emphasis that never breaks flow.** A selection, a click ring and one zoom on the single parameter that matters keep the eye on the right word without any motion graphics. The centre vignette keeps attention in the text column.
4. **Payoff punctuation.** A bass hit on each AI reveal (4×) gives a micro-reward exactly when the viewer waited. All buckets containing a reveal sit at 0.27–0.35, against 0.11–0.15 for talk-over-static buckets.
5. **Proof before instruction.** The chapter opens on the competitor's 954K/617K-view thumbnails before any step. Each claim ("name is free", "topic has no competition") is immediately verified on YouTube and vidIQ.
6. **Where it is weak (to improve, not copy).**
   - Static talk-over-list stretches (05:10–05:16 and 07:23–09:08) are the lowest-replay zones.
   - The 112 s single ChatGPT shot has no zoom, no cut and 7 s holds.
   - The chapter change at 06:41 is invisible.
   - A copy should keep the proof/reveal grammar but add a zoom or cut wherever the screen would sit static for more than 5 s.

## 7. Open questions / low-confidence

- **The click ring may be a recorder feature** (e.g. a cursor-highlight setting), not something added in the edit. Either way it is on every click. Its colour is sampled from anti-aliased thin pixels, so the true hex may be a brighter red (≈ #E53935) at partial opacity.
- **Sub-bass hits:** the harmonic series (≈ 49 Hz) and the 0.2–0.65 s duration fit a cinematic boom or bass-drop SFX. They could also be the first beat of short music stings (analyst #6 calls similar events "music"). I could not hear the audio; classification is from the spectrum only.
- **The vignette** could be a property of the screen-recording tool's canvas rather than the NLE. It is constant and frame-locked either way.
- **Possible micro-cuts during the fast scroll at 03:19–03:26** (scdet 8–10.9 ×6) look like live wheel-scroll and re-sort artefacts, not edits. Low confidence.
- **Font of the "100K Subscribers" card** is not verified; it may be Poppins Black, Montserrat Black or a similar heavy geometric sans.
- **Narration timing comes from Whisper segments**, which can drift by ~0.2–0.5 s, so the cut-lag numbers carry that error.
- **Heatmap is "most replayed",** not retention. Low values mean few replays, not necessarily drop-off.

## Rules to copy

1. **Open every how-to chapter with 10–20 s of proof on the real platform before the first instruction.** Example: a competitor channel sorted by Popular, with 3–4 view counts readable for ≥ 2 s each. Use an ORIGINAL channel or anonymised stand-in where needed. Never open a chapter with a title card.
2. **Record a light-mode browser at 1:1 and keep the native UI.** Cut between apps with hard cuts only: 0 dissolves, 0 whooshes on cuts.
3. **Cut to an app within 0.5–1.5 s after the narrator names it.** Cut on the noun ("PDF", "ChatGPT", "Whisk", "YouTube"). Never cut more than 2.5 s late.
4. **Trim every off-screen action invisibly.** Typing a search query, switching tabs and page loads over 1 s are removed by cutting from frame to near-identical frame, so the result is already on screen. Keep real-time typing only when the typed request itself is the lesson (≤ 8 s, narrated word-for-word as typed).
5. **Use one, and only one, punch-in per chapter.** Apply it to the single parameter the viewer must change (a number in a prompt):
   - scale 1.6–1.8× (target 1.70×), centre-anchored
   - ease-in-out over 1.8 s
   - hold 4–20 s while the value is selected and edited live
   - ease-out over 2.5–3 s before the Send click
   - do not zoom repeat pastes of the same action
6. **Make native text selection the primary highlighter.** Select the exact line or item within ≤ 1.5 s of the narrator naming it. Hold it 1–5 s. Aim for one selection every 7–12 s during list or document screens.
7. **Show a red click-ring on every click.** Thin ring, ~4 % of frame height at max, ~0.3 s expand-and-fade, semi-transparent red (≈ #D32F2F).
8. **Open each sub-step on a styled document heading that acts as the title card.** Red #F7200E bold geometric sans (Lexend Bold class) on a #D7D6D6 highlight bar, ~3 % frame height, centred. Click or select it, then drag-select the whole prompt below it in 2–6 s.
9. **Trim AI waits to ≤ 1 s after Send.** Allow at most one longer wait per chapter (≤ 8 s), and only if the narration is giving a tip over it.
10. **Mark every AI output reveal with a short sub-bass hit.** About 50 Hz fundamental, 0.2–0.7 s, peak ~12–18 dB above speech RMS. Place it in the narration gap of 1–3 s right as the output appears. Use 3–5 per 6 minutes. Never use a continuous music bed under tutorial narration.
11. **Run the voice-over silence-trimmed at 190–210 wpm.** Keep pauses ≤ 0.4 s, except pauses deliberately filled by a reveal hit (≤ 3.3 s).
12. **After each claim about availability or competition, verify it on screen within 5 s.** Search YouTube and show an SEO-tool score panel (e.g. "LOW" competition) for 3–10 s. Leave the real numbers visible.
13. **Never let the frame sit fully static for more than 5 s.** The source has 10 holds ≥ 4 s and two of 7 s, and they align with its lowest replay buckets (0.11–0.15).
    - If talk runs longer over one screen, add a selection change, a scroll, a 1.3–1.5× micro-punch-in, or a cut to a supporting screen every ≤ 5 s.
    - Cap any single uncut app shot at 45 s. The source's 112 s ChatGPT shot is its weakest stretch.
14. **Put the copyable prompt or formula on screen readably and hold it.** Body text ≥ 1.5 % frame height, or zoomed to ≥ 2.5 %. Hold ≥ 3 s per pattern line, selecting each pattern as it is spoken. These are the rewind magnets (range peak 0.449). If the asset is gated, say so on screen.
15. **Apply a fixed radial vignette over all screen recordings.** Centre 100 %, mid-edges about −9 %, corners about −16 % luminance (white → ≈ #D6D6D6). Keep it locked to the frame, not the zoomed content. Keep the 25 px top and bottom letterbox of a 1920×1030 capture.
16. **Use only these on-screen elements in tutorial sections:** native UI, selection, click ring, vignette and the one zoom. No arrows, boxes, emojis, stickers, blur or lower-thirds. Reserve full-screen text cards (bright green #3DFD02 heavy rounded sans, ~10 % frame height, thick black outline and drop shadow, over the page dimmed to ~16 % brightness, 1.5–2 s) for channel CTAs between chapters.
17. **Show results as a quick multi-variant grid, then reject on camera.**
    - Generate 2–4 variants and show them for 3–5 s.
    - Reject openly ("don't like these") up to twice.
    - Then show the fix: type the refinement live and generate again.
    - The good result gets the reveal hit and a visible download within 10 s.
18. **When showing example finance thumbnails or logos, always use an ORIGINAL cartoon character.** Thick black outlines, flat colours, white background, one prop, one curved arrow, 2–4 words of heavy all-caps text. Never reuse or imitate an existing TV-cartoon character or a competitor's mascot.
19. **Mark chapter changes visually.** The source's 06:41 boundary has 0 visual change, and an improvement is cheap. Within 1 s of a new chapter's first line, cut to a new app or Doc heading.
