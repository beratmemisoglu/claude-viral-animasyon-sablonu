## 0. TL;DR — 10 rules

1. **One face, one emotion, one coloured light, one word-sentence. Nothing else.** All three images use exactly four elements [E]: white text, cream face, dark set, single hue light.
2. **The thumbnail shows the *moment of decision*, not the story or the villain.** T1 shows 5:00 with a determined glare, which is the turn at ~1:00, not the opening snooze. T2 shows a hand on the handle before the door opens. T3 shows the moment of consumption, eyes still on the screen. [E: visible]
3. **The text is a verdict sentence with a full stop**, not a label. "DAY 1." / "JUST SHOW UP." / "DOPAMINE." work as declarations. The period turns a word into a statement of finality. [H, consistent across all 3]
4. **The face carries a *negative or effortful* expression**: anger/resolve (T1, T2) or exhausted worry (T3). There are no smiles and no shock faces. This matches large-sample data showing serious/neutral faces lean toward breakouts, smiling slightly underperforms, and exaggerated faces are rare (~5–8%) [E: vidIQ, Eden].
5. **Gaze is the arrow.** In every image the hero's eyes point at the light/object on the right. Text sits top or top-left, so the reading path is text → face → eyes → object. No drawn arrows are needed [E: gaze-cueing works for cartoon faces].
6. **Light-in-darkness = the stakes.** The only saturated hue is the thing the film is about: red clock = time/duty, gold door = opportunity, pink-red phone = temptation. [E: visible; meaning H]
7. **No antagonist in the thumbnail.** The shadow villain, the film's key asset, is absent from all three. The viewer is cast as the villain ("that's me at 5 AM"). This **refutes** the packaging report's guess of "hero vs red shadow on black". [E]
8. **Title and text split the job.** The title names the *topic* (DISCIPLINE = search/identity word). The text names the *action or diagnosis* (DAY 1. = the first step). They are complementary and never repeated.
9. **The dark frame plus one bright light is a pattern interrupt** in a bright, cluttered motivation feed. In dark mode it works *only* because a single high-luminance element (white text, glowing light) pops. A fully dark image disappears. [D]
10. **A/B tests are judged on watch-time share, not CTR** [E: YouTube Help]. The thumbnail must promise exactly what the first 15 s deliver.

## 2. Cross-pattern: the shared "thumbnail brain"

### 2.1 Always present [E: all 3]
1. **One character, face ≥ 1/3 of frame height**, readable at 168×94 px.
2. **One emotion that contains effort or cost**: anger-resolve, fear-resolve, exhaustion-worry.
3. **Eyes pointed at one object** that carries the topic (clock / door / phone).
4. **One light source of one hue family** in a near-black world. The light *is* the stakes.
5. **2–13 characters of white, rounded, heavy caps ending in a period**, top band, never over the face.
6. **A recognisable everyday setting**: bed (×2), front door. These are domestic, private, solitary places where inner struggles happen.
7. **A moment *before* the outcome**: the alarm hasn't been silenced, the door isn't open, the scroll isn't finished. Suspended action is the purest open loop (Zeigarnik-style unfinished task [background]).
8. **The hero faces right/into the frame** with the object on the right, so the reading direction ends on the stakes.

### 2.2 Never present [E: all 3]
- **No villain or shadow twin** (even though it's the film's signature).
- **No second character, no crowd, no background detail** beyond 1–2 props.
- **No arrows, circles, emoji, red boxes, before/after splits, or numbers-as-listicle.**
- **No smiles, no open-mouthed shock face.**
- **No title words in the text**, except T3, the weaker video.
- **No brand logo or channel name.**
- **No question marks or exclamation marks.**

### 2.3 Why it works on mobile and in the suggested feed [H, supported by E where noted]
- **Mobile:** at ~360 px wide, only big shapes survive: one face, one light, 1–3 words. Each image has **≤ 4 regions**, so it degrades gracefully.
- **Suggested feed next to motivation content:** the neighbours are typically live-action gym bodies, Goggins-style speakers, stock B-roll, AI slideshows and yellow-text clickbait. A **cartoon face on black** is a category break (pattern interrupt). It signals "story/film", not "another lecture". The calm period-ended text is the tonal opposite of shouty "!!!" motivation text, which reads as *more credible*. Large-sample data says contrast/brightness are **not** differentiators on average [E: Eden; vidIQ]. So the win is **difference from neighbours**, not contrast per se.
- **Dark-mode UI (#0f0f0f):** a fully dark thumbnail would vanish. These survive because ~15–25% of pixels are high-luminance (white text block, pale face, light). The white text band at the top functions as the thumbnail's "edge" against the dark UI. **Rule: never let the top band go dark.** [D/H]
- **Light-mode UI:** the near-black rectangle is itself the pop, a black hole in a white page. [H]

## 6. A/B testing plan (YouTube Test & Compare)

**Platform facts [E: YouTube Help]**
- Up to **3** thumbnails, or title + thumbnail combinations. Impressions are split evenly.
- The winner is chosen by **watch-time share**, so packaging that over-promises loses even with high CTR.
- Results are "Winner / Preferred / Performed same". A test takes days up to ~2 weeks.
- Near-identical variants run longer and teach nothing.
- Upload at **1280×720 minimum**, or all variants are downscaled to 480p. Desktop Studio only, with advanced features. Not available for Shorts.

**Standard 3-variant structure.** Each variant changes ONE big axis [practitioner advice: 1of10, D].

| Slot | Variant | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| A (default, uploaded first; wins ties) | Concept from §5 with verdict text + twin *hint* | Our house style |
| B | Same image, **text = a different job** (e.g. time stamp vs. the lie vs. no text) | Which text job adds the most |
| C | **Different scene/trigger** (e.g. threshold instead of mirror moment), or "no twin hint" | Which trigger fits this topic |

**Rotation of the test axis across uploads** (to build house rules after ~10 films):
1. Text: verdict vs. time stamp vs. none
2. Twin: hint vs. absent vs. visible (small)
3. Emotion: resolve vs. exhaustion
4. Light hue: warm vs. cool emitter
5. Rendering: flat (film style) vs. painterly glow. This directly tests the T1-vs-T3 question.

**What to log** (one row per video, at 48 h and at day 7):

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Video, topic, title format (A/B/C/D) | |
| Variant IDs + trigger set used (taxonomy #s) | e.g. "1,2,3,5,6,7" |
| Text job (verdict / time / lie / none), word count, period yes/no | |
| Twin presence (none / hint / visible) | |
| Emotion, light hue, render style | |
| T&C result (winner/preferred/same) + watch-time share % per variant | |
| Impressions CTR (48 h, 7 d) vs. channel median | Compare to *own* median, not generic benchmarks |
| AVD %, retention at 0:30 | Checks that the promise matched the first 15 s |
| Traffic mix: Browse / Suggested / Search | Which surface the package works on |
| Top "suggested from" videos | Who are our feed neighbours? |

**Decision rules [H]**
- If a variant has higher CTR but lower watch-time share, it **over-promised**. Fix the thumbnail or the hook, not just the image.
- After 3 consistent wins for one setting on a topic family (fight / loss / fear), make it the default for that family.
- Never test "rounding errors" (font size, slight colour shift).

## 7. Anti-patterns that kill CTR in this niche

| Anti-pattern | Why it hurts | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Repeating the title in the text** (DOPAMINE + "DOPAMINE.") | Wastes the second hook slot | [H], T3 vs T1 |
| **Showing the villain / full blue twin large** | Turns a "this is me" mirror into a "monster video"; shifts genre to horror/fight; the viewer stops seeing himself | [H], absent in all 3 hits |
| **Smiling or happy hero** | Removes tension; no question to answer | [E/D] smiling slightly underperforms (Eden) |
| **Exaggerated shock face / open-mouth scream** | Reads as clickbait; mismatches the calm film tone | [E] rare among breakouts (vidIQ ~5%) |
| **Shouty text: "!!!", "?", all-yellow, 6+ words** | Category-matches the noisy motivation feed instead of breaking it; long text unreadable on mobile | [H] + [E] top breakouts use ≤3 words (vidIQ) |
| **Arrows, red circles, emoji, before/after splits** | Breaks the prestige "film" signal; gaze already directs attention | [H] |
| **Abstract metaphor with no face** (a tree for ROOTS, a mountain) | No self-recognition; nothing for face-detection to grab | [H], ROOTS/READY underperformed (catalog) |
| **All-dark image with no bright element** | Vanishes in dark-mode UI | [D] |
| **Multiple light colours** | Destroys the "one thing matters" read | [H] |
| **Thumbnail scene that isn't in the first 15 s** | Intro drop-off, so it loses on watch-time share | [E] T&C metric; YouTube intro guidance |
| **Using a raw film frame** | Hero too small (~12–25% of height), letterbox and DAY counter clutter | [E] frame #2 vs T1 |
| **Copying the competitor's red/bean/door-light 1:1** | Brand confusion plus the YPP "inauthentic/repetitive" risk; viewers may think it's a reupload | [H] + packaging report S7–S8 |
| **Twin-blue as the dominant colour in every thumbnail** | Makes every film look identical in the feed and kills topic differentiation | [H]; keep blue as a hint, vary the emitter hue per topic |
