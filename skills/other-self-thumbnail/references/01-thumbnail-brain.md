# 01 — The Thumbnail Brain (why people click, and the rules that follow)

Sources: `deep-research/02-click-psychology.md` (taxonomy, per-thumbnail reads, concepts, A/B) and
`deep-research/01-visual-forensics.md` (measurements). Labels: [E] evidence/measured · [H] hypothesis.

## 1. The formula (all 3 viral thumbnails, measured)

**ONE face · ONE effortful emotion · eyes on ONE object · ONE coloured light in a near-black world · 1–3 white
words ending in a period.** Nothing else. [E]

It shows **the moment just before a decision** (alarm not yet off, hand on the handle, scroll not yet over) —
not the story, not the villain. The viewer becomes the villain: "that's me at 5 AM". [E/H]

## 2. Always / never

| Always | Never |
|---|---|
| Hero close-up: head 34–49 % of frame height (≈4× closer than the film) | The shadow antagonist / full blue twin (0/3 hits show it) |
| Effort or cost on the face: anger-resolve, fear-resolve, exhaustion | Smiles, open-mouth shock, screaming |
| Gaze → the object → reading path text → face → light | Arrows, circles, emoji, red boxes, before/after splits |
| One emitter hue = the stakes (clock, door gap, phone) | Two light colours, busy backgrounds, crowds, logos |
| Domestic private place (bed, door, desk, kitchen at night) | Abstract metaphor with no face (ROOTS/READY underperformed) |
| White heavy rounded caps + period (or "?": IS THIS IT? / WHAT IF?), top band, never over the face | "!", 6+ words, yellow shouty text |
| The scene appears in the film's first 15 s or at the turn | Raw film frames (hero too small, letterbox/DAY clutter) |

## 3. Text — the second hook

- **Title and text split the job.** Title = the topic/pain word people search ("DISCIPLINE | An Animated Short
  Film"). Text = the action, time or lie ("DAY 1."). Never repeat the title word (DOPAMINE + "DOPAMINE." was the
  weaker one). [E/H]
- **Text jobs** (pick one): the first step ("DAY 1.", "JUST START.") · a time stamp ("3:07 AM.", "8:47.") ·
  the voice's lie said calmly ("TOMORROW.", "STAY.", "ONE MORE.") · the scene name ("TABLE FOR ONE.") · the vow
  ("DON'T CALL.").
- **The period** turns a word into a verdict/declaration and kills hype. Calm = credible next to shouty
  motivation thumbnails. [H]

## 4. Click-trigger taxonomy (use 4–6 per thumbnail)

1 **Mirror moment** — a private ritual the viewer does but never sees from outside.
2 **Suspended decision** — one frame before the choice resolves.
3 **Light = stakes** — the only saturated thing is the temptation or the opportunity.
4 **Gaze vector** — eyes point at the object; no drawn arrows needed.
5 **Effortful emotion** — cost on the face (fight → resolve; loss → exhaustion/longing; fear → sweat + resolve).
6 **Verdict text with period.**
7 **Complementary pair** — title = topic noun, text = step/time/lie.
8 **Ticking clock** — a visible time or counter.
9 **Threshold** — door, window, edge, stairs (change is possible).
10 **Forbidden inside view** — show the hidden mechanism (exposed brain, thoughts made visible). For us: the blue
   twin's glow *is* the inside view — use only as a hint.
11 **Category break** — cartoon on black among live-action/stock/AI-slideshow motivation thumbnails.

## 5. Brand rules for The Other Self

- Star: Moon head (`inner-voice-film` skill → `assets/brand-the-other-self/`). On-model always: sprout, tall oval
  eyes with highlight, sand body, thin ink outline.
- **Sprout = emotional barometer** at thumbnail size: upright = hope/resolve, drooping = defeat, curled = anxiety,
  a new leaf = day 1. [H]
- **Emitter hue follows the topic metaphor** (amber clock/door = time/opportunity, cool white-cyan phone = screen,
  warm lamp = loneliness at a table, cold moonlight = 3 AM). The competitor's red is never our default.
- **Brand blue `#2F6BFF` is a HINT only**: a blue reflection in the phone glass/window/mirror, a blue-rimmed shadow
  on the wall, a small twin far in the corner. Test "hint vs none" in A/B. If every thumbnail were blue, films
  would be indistinguishable in the feed.
- Font: Fredoka 700 (fallback Baloo 2 800 / Nunito 900), white, dark stroke ≈7.5 % of size, soft shadow.

## 6b. Hook Engine — MANDATORY before rendering (lesson from 2026-10-14 tests)

A correct-but-literal thumbnail ("hero in bed + clock") was judged by the user as **not eye-catching, not
clickable** even after a high-quality 3D finish. The fix was the *idea*, not the rendering. For every film,
write **4 concepts, one per hook type**, then score them and render the top 2–3:

| Hook type | What it is | Tested example (procrastination) |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 Absurd exaggeration** | the habit multiplied to an impossible scale | buried under dozens of ringing alarm clocks, lazily pressing snooze on the top one — "TOMORROW." |
| **H2 Inner voice made visible** | the blue twin physically doing the sabotage (hands over his eyes, holding the blanket down, pulling the phone closer) | twin covers his eyes from behind, calm smile — "NOT TODAY." ← strongest |
| **H3 Surreal metaphor** | a Dalí-like impossible object that *is* the problem | melting neon clock dripping onto the pillow while he sleeps smiling — "LATER." |
| **H4 Extreme-emotion close-up** | face fills half the frame, one saturated light, the emotion at its peak | 3 AM cold cyan phone light, wide exhausted eyes — "ONE MORE." |

**Stop-scroll score** (0–2 each, render only concepts ≥ 8/12):
1 Is there a "wait, what?" in < 0.5 s (something impossible, exaggerated or unexpected)?
2 Is one SATURATED colour popping on near-black (neon cyan/magenta/blue/amber at full saturation — not a dim brown)?
3 Is the emotion strong (not "oops"/cute) and readable at 168 px?
4 Does the image tell the film's conflict in one frame (temptation + victim)?
5 Does the viewer recognise themselves (bed, phone, alarm, table for one)?
6 Is it different from what the competitor and the feed show?

**H2 note:** the twin as the active saboteur beat the "no villain" finding in our test — the competitor never had a
twin concept; for us it is the brand. Rule: the twin may appear when it *does* the temptation to the hero (not as a
big standalone monster), it must match the hero's silhouette (same round head + sprout), and the hero stays the
larger, central face. A/B-test H2 vs the best other hook.

**Drift risk rises with boldness** (2 of 4 bold concepts went off-model: double-leaf sprout, eye whites/irises,
gibberish clock text "SNOZE"). For bold concepts: generate 2–3 candidates each, describe the character lock
explicitly, forbid "eye whites, irises, pupils with whites, two leaves, any text on objects", and re-check on-model.

## 7. Why DISCIPLINE's thumbnail likely won (ranked, n=1 — treat as bets)

Biggest audience pool · complementary text–title pair · high-arousal emotion (anger-resolve) vs tired worry ·
simplest image (flat, 3 objects) · promises transformation, not explanation · matches the film's first 15 s.
Note: the 3.1M thumbnail is the *least* rendered (flat) — polish did not drive the click; composition, emotion
and the text–title pair did.
