**The formula in one sentence:** an original sad-sack everyman, shown in his daily pain within 6 s, wants one thing. The world blocks him in 3–8 escalating rungs, each a fresh archetype or location. Pantomime carries a third of the film and phones and signs carry the exposition. A setup from the first half is paid off or inverted in the last fifth. His circumstances end unchanged, so the viewer leaves with a feeling (recognition, dread, hope) and a story of their own to post.

## 4. Escalation ladder: how each rung beats the last

**Ladder types observed:**

1. **Archetype ladder** (job videos). Each rung is a new archetype with a higher absurdity or a higher cost to the protagonist.
   - v03: indecisive driver (75 s) → coupon-after-payment → contradictory boss → 98-vs-100 fries complainer who summons **his mother** (a bigger antagonist) → relief: nice customers → bullies who throw a drink.
   - v06: Karen (52 s) → 3 interruptions (each ≈15 s) → hero stolen → rival steals the break → rival dumps work → manager's unfair scolding → rival's delusion → the boyfriend.
2. **Loss ladder** (v01). Each rung removes one thing: the girl → friends (car seats emptying) → a job prospect → dignity (friend's success seen from the drive-thru) → father (hospital, empty bed) → last friend → love (betrayal). That is **7 losses in 190 s, one per ≈27 s**, and each loss is bigger than the last; death sits at the 2/3 point and betrayal at 84 %.
3. **Habit ladder** (v02, v04). Each rung is one habit, ordered from private to public: room/sleep → sobriety → quitting smoking → reading → talking to people → gym → being seen (mirror, restaurant). v04 inserts **a failure at rung 2** (run → collapse, 0:57–1:01) and **a reset** (whiteboard, 1:02).
4. **Points/counter ladder** (v07). A visible score drops: 648 → stays (forced choice) → 650 (gaming the system) → 645 → foil −500. The counter keeps the escalation measurable on screen.
5. **Puppet-master ladder** (v08). An external god issues 6 commands in 44 s (rain → far order → again → pressure → mood → blind driver). Each command line is 1–2 s and its on-screen effect 2–4 s, so **one rung every ≈7 s**: the fastest ladder in the corpus.
6. **Phase ladder** (v10). Labelled stages with dramatic irony: the viewer reads where it's going before the protagonist does.

**Measured spacing:** rung intervals are 7 s (v08 commands), 15 s (v06 interruptions), 27 s (v01 losses) and 50–75 s (v03/v06 vignettes). Shorter rungs mean wordless or near-wordless delivery. **A single rung longer than ≈45 s on reused plates underperforms**: v06 Karen (52 s) has the lowest replay of that film (0.009–0.05).

**Rung-escalation rule observed:** each rung must raise **one** of {absurdity, personal cost, public visibility, antagonist size}, and the ladder must turn at least once against the protagonist when he finally acts (v06 3:35 hero stolen; v08 1:58 tempting fate then punishment; v01 3:05 betrayal right after his sacrifice purchase).

## 11. Dialogue craft (voiced videos)

- **Line length.** Sentence medians are 5–9 words; p90 is 12–20; **26–54 % of sentences are ≤5 words**; questions are 13–26 %. Long sentences (≥15 words) are reserved for antagonists' rants and systems' notices (v07 25 %, v05 16 %), never for the protagonist's emotions.
- **Who speaks.**
  - The protagonist is the **minority speaker** (v02 33 %, v06 ≈27 %).
  - Talk comes from the people who act on him: customers, bosses, systems, mentors, foils.
  - His inner voice (same performer, same ≈95–108 Hz pitch) is used sparingly (v06 has 3 inner lines, ≤15 words each) and always **right before a block**.
- **Register.**
  - Plain, everyday, slightly formal non-native English: uncontracted verbs and literal idioms, which read as deadpan.
  - Meme vocabulary is sprinkled in, ≤1 per 30 s: rat race, wage cage, top G, ascend, bro.
  - Profanity is rare (≤2 per film).
- **How exposition is delivered or avoided:**
  1. **System voice / UI notification** carries the premise (v05, v07).
  2. **A confrontation forces the rules out** (the Karen states the prices; the government-gym receptionist states the rules).
  3. **A mentor's Socratic questions** make the protagonist state his own problem (v02 1:16).
  4. **A coworker's small talk** carries the backstory (v06 crypto, v03 hand-offs).
  5. **A foil's long argument** in one static location carries the theme (v07 car 2+ min, v09 bar debate 108 s, v05 locker 84 s). This is the one place the channel tolerates 20–44 s static holds.
- **Rhetorical devices:**
  - **Socratic self-answer** (v02 1:16)
  - **echo-correction** (v02 1:38–1:41)
  - **antithesis** either/or (v02 0:37)
  - **aphorism ≤12 words** on its own camera angle (v02 2:38)
  - **triad** of gratitude (v02 4:02)
  - **countdown** (v06 2:24)
  - **tempting fate** (v08 1:58)
  - **meta-concession** (v02 3:38)
  - **delusion ladder** (v06 6:29)
  - **callback line transfer** (v06 7:21)
  - **"You're X, right?" age jab** (v06 6:23)
  - **rhetorical how-awful anaphora** ×4 (v07 4:41–4:59)
- **Quotable lines.** Hope/dystopia scripts contain 2–4 aphorisms of ≤12 words that viewers turn into quote-comments (v02: 6.4 k, 2.5 k, 90; v04: viewers supply their own aphorisms, 12 k and 4.4 k). **Viewers even invent a "quote" the script never said** (v02 6.4 k), which shows the theme was stated clearly enough to be condensed.

## 14. (b) Wordless-script writing method (a producer can follow it)

**Step 1: Logline as a state change.** "[Original character] goes from A to B because of C," e.g. hopeful → hopeless because of 7 losses.

**Step 2: Status-marker sheet.** List 6–10 visible markers and when each flips:
- costume (beanie, uniform)
- face drawing (eye bags, tears, colour flush, jaw design)
- room state (messy/clean)
- held prop (pill, bottle, phone, controller)
- companions in frame (seats filling or emptying)
- sky/grade (night → dawn)

Each marker flips ≥1 time; the main one flips at 50–60 %.

**Step 3: Escalation ladder.** Write 5–8 rungs, one sentence each. One location per rung, 8–20 s per rung. Each rung raises exactly one of {absurdity, cost, visibility, antagonist size}. Mark the rung where the protagonist finally acts, and make the world answer that action badly (or, in a rise story, with a relapse).

**Step 4: Shot grammar per rung (EER).**
- **E**stablish: WS, 2–3 s.
- **E**vent: insert or action, 1–3 s.
- **R**eaction: CU, 1–3 s; hold 4–6 s for emotional rungs.

Gags use the visual flip (same framing, 1 s + 1 s) or the list (3–4 items × 1.5 s).

**Step 5: Text budget.** Exposition only through diegetic text: phone UI, billboard, whiteboard, news headline, sign, app ad.
- ≤12 words per element, on screen ≥1 s per 4 words.
- 4–8 text elements per film, ≤1 per 10 s.
- Never a narrator caption, except a time caption ("Later…") or phase cards.

**Step 6: Time devices.** Alarm/clock flip, clock hands, exterior sky grade, costume change, a 1–5 s black card between acts (v02 2 s, v08 5 s).

**Step 7: Callbacks.** Choose 2–4 images to repeat later with one variable changed: same plate with a new state, or the same prop now unused.

**Step 8: Music map.** Write as a column in the shot list:
- silence or a single pad for 0–3 s
- the track starts on the first event
- a drop or stop at the turn
- the peak at the transformation or reveal
- a fade to silence under the last 4 s
- the end card silent or a stinger

**Step 9: Shot-list format.**

| # | t | plate (stock/illustrated) | character state (markers) | action | insert/text | music cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Step 10: Mute test + logline test.**
- Mute test: with audio off, a first-time viewer must be able to say what changed in every rung.
- Logline test: the end state must keep one original lack visible.

**Worked micro-example** (original character "Tomo", a tired night-shift nurse; first 40 s):

## 17. (e) Anti-patterns (evidence-based)

1. **A verbal list with no picture.** v02 1:21–1:32 (complaints) has its lowest replay (0.17–0.20). Every listed item needs a ≤1.5 s insert.
2. **One escalation stretched past 45 s on reused plates.** The v06 Karen (52 s, 3 plates, 4.4 s median shot) has the lowest replay of the film.
3. **Setup without payoff.** v06's crypto/escape thread (25 s) never returns.
4. **Solving the protagonist's material problem.** This never happens in the top 10 (0/10). A clean win would break the recognition contract.
5. **A narrator explaining the moral.** This never happens. Morals arrive through a character (mentor), an in-world chorus (social posts) or are implied.
6. **The protagonist explaining his feelings.** His big emotions are silent CUs; his inner voice speaks only before a block.
7. **The first conflict after 45 s.** 9/10 have it earlier; the exception (v09, 62 s) compensates with a stylish hook and a news peg.
8. **Runtime creep.** No top-10 video exceeds 454 s. The channel's "Life of" uploads went from 211–453 s (2022–23, 1.4–4.9 M) to 480–647 s (2025–26, 0.14–0.62 M). This is confounded by channel age, but no evidence supports going longer.
9. **Static 20–44 s holds outside foil-debate scenes.** The holds work only when the dialogue itself is the escalation (v06 rival, v07 car, v09 debate).
10. **Twist told in dialogue.** Never; reveals are ≤5 s cutaways.
11. **Real brands, real people, real works.** These are present in the corpus (golden-arches look-alikes, a big-box logo look-alike, a book cover, a meme photo of a real person, real game footage) but are an IP/likeness risk for us.
12. **Stereotype-as-punchline** (body type, disability). It works in comments but carries risk; make the behaviour the joke.

## 18. Authored vs generated: which script mechanics come from a writer?

| Component | Verdict | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall structure, ladders, callbacks, long-range setups/payoffs | **Authored by one human writer-director** | Pinned/uploader comments are first-person about story intent and effort: v01 says the serious story needed extra editing time to feel cinematic; v05 apologises for a 3-week gap caused by the workload; v09 announces a deliberately serious departure. Setups are paid off 2–5 min later (v06 ×6). A cross-video universe (v01→v02 same plates; the same jet villain v07/v08; the same crypto living room in 4 videos; parody brands reused). | high |
| Dialogue wording | **Human, non-native (likely Dutch-speaking)** | Description slip "de sequel" (v02). A Dutch delivery brand referenced (v08). Uncontracted, literal phrasing throughout. A subtitle typo (v06 3:51). An LLM draft would be idiomatic and typo-free. | medium-high |
| Advice and world-building content (v02 self-help list; v05 steroid-legalisation backstory) | **Possibly researched/listicle-derived, possibly LLM-assisted from 2023** | Generic, list-like, explained in long sentences (v05 16 % of sentences ≥15 words). There is no direct artefact. | low |
| Voices | **Unknown** | ≥5 consistent pitch profiles; the protagonist's voice is identical across v02 and v06 (≈94–108 Hz). Human vs synthetic is not testable on 16 kHz mono. | – |
| Backgrounds | **Stock photographs + some painted/possibly AI-generated plates** (the v02/v04 night hilltop, the restaurant) | Characters are flat cut-outs on single plates; the same plates recur across videos | low-medium |
| Character art | **Authored, template-based** (one base face, swappable expression drawings, costume layers) | The same CU drawings recur; state changes are done by drawing swaps and dissolves | medium |

**Implication for our skill:** generation can safely draft settings and checklists (stations, archetype lists, accuracy details). The **ladder ordering, the setup→payoff map, the withheld item and the ending's unchanged lack must be designed explicitly**; these are the authored parts that the replay peaks reward.

## Why it works

1. **Recognition before story.** Every film opens on a pain the viewer has lived (alarm, forced smile, empty room, angry customer), shown, not told, within 6 s. Recognition is the hook, and comments become testimony (job videos: 1.4–1.7 comments per 1k views, dominated by I-worked-at stories).
2. **A simple want against a ladder of blockers**, each rung a fresh archetype, location or absurdity. That keeps novelty high while the emotional line stays simple.
3. **Pantomime does the heavy lifting.** A quarter to all of each film is wordless, the protagonist's peaks are silent close-ups, and exposition lives in phones and signs. The films travel across languages: v01/v04 are wordless and among the top 4.
4. **Planted payoffs in the last fifth**: reveals, callbacks, inverted repeats. The top replay moment sits at 77–97 % of the runtime in 8/10.
5. **Honest endings.** Nothing material is fixed, so hope feels earned (v02/v04 at 4.36 % likes), dread feels plausible (v09 5.49 % likes, 2.7 comments per 1k), and comedy stays relatable.
6. **Universe continuity.** A recurring everyman, recurring plates and brands, and sequels that open on the previous cliffhanger turn single videos into lore (v01 lore comment, 16 k likes).
