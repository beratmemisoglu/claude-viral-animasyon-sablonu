## 0. TL;DR — the formula in one paragraph

A silent, unnamed bean hero repeats a **daily micro-ritual** (the 5:00 alarm). A red-glowing **inner voice** talks him out of it, using short lowercase lines. **DAY counters** jump fast while he decays. The voice then becomes **his own reflection**. At **rock bottom** under a spotlight, the narrator (white text) **reframes his identity**: "X isn't who you are. It's what you repeat." The **same ritual shot** comes back, and this time he makes the opposite choice. That choice starts a new **DAY 1**. In the **grind montage**, the day counters climb, and each old red line is **physically defeated** (he pushes through it, punches it, or walks through it). The voice comes back as a **giant final boss** and gets split in two. At the **summit** a white **lesson couplet** lands. A **bookend** repeats the opening shot with the voice now **tiny and powerless**. The film ends on **one title word**, which is also the theme, and a **chiasmus slogan** ("Day one, or one day.").

Story Spine version (Adams): *Every day* he snoozed → *But one day* he believed the voice → *Because of that* he hit bottom → *Until finally* the same alarm rang and he stood up → *Ever since then* the voice is tiny.

### 1C. Why DISCIPLINE beat DOPAMINE by ~11× (hypotheses for the skill)

1. **Hook:** DISCIPLINE opens on a *failure everyone shares* (snooze, "again.") within 13 s. DOPAMINE opens on *happiness*, which gives no pain to recognize and no question to keep watching for.
2. **Antagonist voice:** DISCIPLINE gives the shadow **6+ distinct, quotable lines**, each defeated later. DOPAMINE has essentially one ("just one more") plus a graph HUD, so there are fewer callbacks to pay off.
3. **Physical payoffs:** words shattered by punches, a giant split in two. DOPAMINE's boss is just turned away from, which is less cathartic.
4. **Rock bottom length:** DISCIPLINE spends 10 s at the bottom with an *identity reframe*. DOPAMINE's crash runs 43 s with a statistic-like line and no "who you are" reframe.
5. **Slogan:** "Day one, or one day." is a perfect chiasmus with a decision. "Cheap pleasure steals your color. Earn it back." is longer and has no mirrored structure.
6. **Bookend object:** the alarm clock is a *time* object, so it also carries the DAY-counter idea. A grass meadow carries less meaning.
→ **Default to the DISCIPLINE template.** Use DOPAMINE's "paradise lost" opener only when the topic is about *losing* something you once had (dopamine, grief, heartbreak), and even then keep the failure stamp inside the first 15 s.

## 3. THE RULES (what makes it work)

### R1. Bookending: same shot, opposite choice
- The **ritual shot appears 3 times**: B01 (fail), B07 (choose), B12 (own it). The framing, props and clock position are identical across all three. Only these change: **the action** (slap/snooze → instant kill → already awake), **the light** (moon → none → dawn), **the token** (none → appears → worn), and **the shadow** (absent → absent → tiny).
- The ritual object must **display a number** (clock time, unread count, mug count). Numbers read instantly, need no translation, and echo the DAY counter.
- In the hook card, the word "same" ("Every day started the same.") is a setup the bookend pays off: by the end, the day starts *differently*.

### R2. Callbacks: every voice line is later defeated physically
Keep a **Voice Ledger**. Each red line in Act 1 must be paid off in Act 2:

| Act-1 line (DISCIPLINE) | Act-2 payoff |
|---|---|
| "just 5 more minutes..." | B07: alarm killed in 0 s |
| "skip the gym." | B08 G1–G3: he trains every day |
| "you're tired." | G1 run: he's exhausted, stops, and runs again |
| "you're lazy. you'll never change." | G3: "you're lazy" punched into flying letters · G4: mirror reflection now smiles |
| (new) "told you." | G2: one more rep, counter "3" |
| (new) "you can't." | B10: he walks through it and it splits |

Rule: **the hero never answers verbally. He answers with an action** that contradicts the line, in the same shot as the line.

### R3. Antagonist escalation curve (size + distance + intimacy)
| Stage | Beat | Form | Size vs hero | Position | Line type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Invisible | B02 | Red text only, coming "from" the hero | — | above hero | rationalization ("just 5 more minutes...") |
| 1 Whisperer | B03 | Full shadow, raised hand | 1.0–1.1× | behind furniture | imperative ("skip the gym.") |
| 2 Companion | B04 | Leaning on his chair, smiling | 1.1× | touching his space | fake empathy ("you're tired.") |
| 3 Reflection | B05 | Replaces his mirror image | 1.0× | *is* him | identity + prophecy ("you're lazy. you'll never change.") |
| 4 Absent | B06 | Gone. *He* now believes it | — | — | narrator: "He believed it." |
| 5 Disembodied | B08 | Red words only | text 2–3× | floating | taunts ("told you.", label lists) |
| 6 Giant | B10 | Huge silhouette, arms wide | 3–5× | blocking the path | absolute negation ("you can't.") |
| 7 Tiny | B12 | Shrunken, dim glow, head down | ≈0.3× | corner of the room | silent |

Escalation in Act 1 runs on **closeness**; in Act 2 it runs on **size**. The voice's power is drawn as **glow intensity** (brightest at B10, dimmest at B12). Its face is always the same: closed smiling arc-eyes plus a smile. **It never looks angry, only amused.** That smugness is what makes defeating it satisfying.

### R4. Voice-line grammar (red)
- Lowercase, **1–4 words**, ending with a period ("skip the gym." "told you." "you can't."). The period matters: it makes each line a *verdict*, not a question. (Exception: the OVERTHINKING voice speaks in "what if…?" questions.)
- Escalation ladder (use in this order): **rationalization → imperative → fake empathy → identity label → prophecy → "I told you so" → label list → absolute negation.**
- The final-boss line is the **shortest line in the film**.

### R5. Lesson lines are 2-part couplets (white)
Each white narrator beat is **two lines shown one after the other** (line 2 appears ~1.2–1.5 s after line 1, with line 1 still visible or fading). Types:
| Type | Pattern | DISCIPLINE instance |
|---|---|---|
| Fact / duration | "He ___ it." / "Day after day." | "He believed it." / "Day after day." |
| Identity reframe | "But X isn't who you are." / "It's what you ___." | "...lazy..." / "...repeat." |
| Parallel antithesis (theme) | "[A] gets you ___." / "[TITLE] keeps you ___." | Motivation / Discipline |
| Hard truth → empowerment | "Nobody is coming to ___ you." / "So ___ yourself." | save / save |
| Chiasmus slogan | "A B, or B A." | "Day one, or one day." |
Couplet rule: **line 2 reuses a key word or structure of line 1 and turns it around** (save → save yourself; gets you → keeps you; day one → one day).

### R6. Narrator pronoun shift = turning to the viewer
Act 1 narration is **3rd person, past tense** ("Every day started the same." "He believed it."). At the reframe it switches to **2nd person, present tense** ("lazy isn't who *you* are"). From then on every white line says "you". The film stops describing him and starts addressing the viewer. Keep this shift in every adaptation, at the same moment (B06 line 3).

### R7. Title word = theme = final card
- The title is **one abstract noun, ALL CAPS** (DISCIPLINE, DOPAMINE, LONELINESS, READY, ROOTS). YouTube title: `WORD | An Animated Short Film`.
- The word appears in **exactly two places**: inside lesson couplet 1 (B09) and on the end card (B13). Nowhere else. The B09 mention primes the card.
- The title is either **the cure** (DISCIPLINE) or **the battlefield** (DOPAMINE, LONELINESS). If it's the battlefield, couplet 1 names the cure explicitly.

### R8. Chiasmus / antimetabole slogan
- Form: **"X Y, or Y X."** or **"[verb] it, or it [verb]s you."** At most 6 words, ideally a binary choice. Optional tail: "You decide."
- Test: can it be printed on a T-shirt, and does reversing it change the meaning? If yes to both, it works.

### R9. Universal, silent, unnamed protagonist
- No name, no dialogue, no voice-over. Emotion comes from **brows + eyes + mouth + posture** only. Gender is implied as "he" in narration. A neutral "they" variant is allowed, but stay consistent within a film.
- No speech bubbles from the hero ever. *All* text is either the voice (red), the narrator (white), or system info (gray counters / white rep numbers).
- The setting is a generic modern life (bed, couch, desk, mirror, city, stairs), with no brands, no country markers, and no faces of other people beyond simple beans. Side characters (DOPAMINE's friend) get one hat-type identifier only.

### R10. DAY counters
- Gray, top-left, "DAY N" in uppercase, shown on **every scene of B03–B10**. Not shown on the hook, the rock bottom, the summit, the bookend or the card. Those are timeless moments.
- **Decay counters** (B03–B05): DAY 1 → big irregular jumps → 365. Time is *wasted*, so the jumps feel careless (1, 25, 47, 88, 140, 210, 365).
- **Grind counters** (B07–B10): **restart at DAY 1** (the second DAY 1 is a plot point), then climb in a roughly geometric progression (3, 12, 64, 150, 190, 270). Small numbers early show that the beginning is hard; big numbers late show the progress compounding.
- A **second counter type** is allowed in grind scenes: big glowing white **rep/attempt numbers** center-top ("3"). They mark effort *within* a day.

### R11. Text color = speaker (non-negotiable)
| Role | Color | Case | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| NARRATOR / LESSON | white/cream #e8e4d8 | Sentence case | "He believed it." |
| VOICE (antagonist) | red #ff3b30 (glow) | lowercase | "you can't." |
| INFO-FAIL (internal state / verdict) | red | lowercase | "just 5 more minutes..." "again." |
| COUNTER (time) | gray #9a9a9a | UPPERCASE | "DAY 64" |
| REP (effort) | white, glowing, large | digits | "3" |
| TITLE | cream + red wavy underline | UPPERCASE | "DISCIPLINE" |

### R12. Other invariants
- **Palette as plot:** monochrome + red for the whole film. Blue (phone) means seduction, amber (lamp/streetlight) means effort, and **orange dawn appears only once, at the summit**. In DOPAMINE, color itself *is* the stakes. For "lost-something" topics (grief, heartbreak, dopamine), use desaturation-to-gray as the decay meter.
- **Music in 4 phases:** sad piano (B00–B06) → heartbeat thump transition (B07) → driving drums/synth (B08–B10) → swell + wind (B11) → calm resolve (B12–B13).
- **Effort must be visible:** sweat drop, gritted teeth, shaking limbs, a stop-and-restart. Never show effortless success.
- **One antagonist, one hero.** No dialogue scenes, no plot twists, no side quests.
- **Sensitive topics:** the voice may be cruel, but the film must never depict self-harm. The reframe must always restore agency.

## 4. TOPIC ADAPTATION ENGINE

Input: `TITLE` (one word) + `TOPIC` (one line). Output: the filled 13-beat sheet. Do steps 1–12 in order. Each step has a test that must pass.

| Step | Produce | How / formula | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Core struggle** | One sentence: "The hero keeps choosing **[easy thing]** instead of **[hard thing]** because a voice says **[lie]**." | Name the comfortable avoidance behavior behind the topic. | Can a 14-year-old and a 45-year-old both say "that's me"? |
| **2. Daily micro-ritual** | Location + **object showing a number** + a time of day + the easy choice | Choose the moment in the day when the struggle is decided (wake-up, coming home, bedtime, morning coffee, first phone check). | Is the object drawable in under 10 SVG shapes, and does it show a number? |
| **3. Antagonist identity** | "The voice of ___" + what it *protects* the hero from | The voice personifies the avoidance. It always pretends to *protect* (comfort, safety, loyalty, rest). | Does the voice sound kind at first and cruel later? |
| **4. False comfort + failure stamp** | Red rationalization (≤5 words, with "..."), then a one-word red verdict | The rationalization uses "just" / "one more" / "later". The verdict is "again." or "still." or "alone." | Does the verdict work as a single word? |
| **5. Voice ledger (6–10 lines)** | The lines in ladder order: rationalization → imperative → fake empathy → identity label → prophecy → "told you." → label list (3 words) → absolute negation | Lowercase, 1–4 words, period. Each must be payable by one physical action in the grind. | Is every line paired with a grind payoff? |
| **6. Decay vignettes (3–5)** | Short scenes of the avoidance spreading into life | Rotate rooms: couch → desk → kitchen → bed at night. Counters jump irregularly. | Does each vignette show a *different* life area? |
| **7. Mirror line** | "you're [label]. you'll never [change/be ___]." | The label is the identity the topic makes people believe about themselves. | Is the label a self-belief people actually hold (lazy, unlovable, broken, behind, weak)? |
| **8. Rock-bottom image + reframe couplets** | A: "He [verb-ed] it." / "Day after day." (or "Night after night.") · B: "But [label] isn't who you are." / "It's what you [behavior verb]." | The reframe converts the **identity** (noun/adjective) into a **behavior** (verb), which makes it changeable. | Does line B2 end on a verb the grind then reverses? |
| **9. Bookend change + identity token** | What he does *instantly* at the same moment in B07, plus one visible token he gains (headband, scarf, earbuds, apron, plant) | The opposite of the B01 action, done in under 1 s, without hesitation. | Can the token be a single red-accent shape that is readable at small size? |
| **10. Grind activities (4–6)** | G1 struggle-and-continue · G2 callback beaten by "one more" + rep counter · G3 words destroyed physically · G4 proof (calendar) + mirror reversal · (G5/G6 optional) | Each activity is the *active opposite* of a decay vignette. G3 needs a "destroy" verb (punch, tear, wash off, erase, cut, blow away). | Does each grind scene mirror a decay scene? |
| **11. Lesson couplet 1 + final boss** | "[Fake fix] gets you ___." / "[TITLE] keeps you ___." (or a variant naming the cure) · Boss = the giant voice blocking the path, with the *shortest* line | The boss blocks a **literal threshold** (stairs, door, stage, starting line). The hero passes *through* it. | Is the boss line ≤3 words? Does the hero never stop moving? |
| **12. Summit couplet + slogan** | Hard truth / empowerment, reusing a word · chiasmus ≤6 words + "You decide." (optional) | Summit couplet: "Nobody can ___ for you." / "So ___ yourself." family. Slogan: "X Y, or Y X." | Could the slogan be on a T-shirt? Does it reverse meaningfully? |

**Final QA checklist:** the hook is under 4 s · first failure stamp before 0:15 · DAY 1 shown twice · rock bottom at 31–40% · the pronoun switches to "you" at the reframe · every red line has a payoff · the title word appears only in B09 and B13 · B01/B07/B12 framing identical · warm palette only at summit/bookend · the hero never speaks.

## 6. SCREENPLAY OUTPUT TEMPLATE (the skill must write scripts in exactly this format)

### 6.1 Header
```
TITLE: DISCIPLINE
YT_TITLE: DISCIPLINE | An Animated Short Film
LOGLINE: A man fights the voice that keeps telling him "tomorrow."
RUNTIME: 160s @ 30fps = 4800 frames | 1920x1080
THEME_COUPLET: "Motivation gets you started." / "Discipline keeps you going."
SLOGAN: "Day one, or one day."
RITUAL: bedroom | alarm clock | 05:00 | easy choice = snooze | new choice = kill alarm instantly
TOKEN: red headband (appears B07)
ANTAGONIST: "the voice of tomorrow" | protects him from: discomfort
VOICE_LEDGER:
  V1 "just 5 more minutes..."  -> paid B07 (alarm killed in 0s)
  V2 "skip the gym."            -> paid G1 (run)
  V3 "you're tired."            -> paid G1 (stops, runs again)
  V4 "you're lazy. you'll never change." -> paid G3 (punch shatter), G4 (mirror smile)
  V5 "told you."                -> paid G2 (rep counter 3)
  V6 "you can't."               -> paid B10 (split)
PALETTE_ARC: mono+red -> blue phone (B03) -> amber lamp (B04/G1) -> orange dawn (B11) -> dawn window (B12)
MUSIC: M1 sad piano | M2 heartbeat thump | M3 drive (drums/synth) | M4 swell+wind | M5 calm outro
```

### 6.2 Scene block (one per shot)
```
### S07 — B04 DECAY MONTAGE (2/4)
FRAMES:      660–870   (22.0s–29.0s, 210f)
SETTING:     desk_room | wall_clock 7:05 | desk lamp ON (amber cone) | book stack | floor line y=980
COUNTER:     "DAY 47" (COUNTER, gray, top-left, fade-in 8f)
CHARACTERS:
  HERO   pos=(1060,980) pose=slumped_desk_reach  expr=tired_halflid  token=none
  SHADOW pos=(420,980)  pose=lean_on_chair       expr=smug_arc       scale=1.1 glow=0.6
ACTION:
  0–40f    HERO arms slide forward across desk, head sinks (ease-in-out)
  40–90f   SHADOW drifts in from left 60px, right hand settles on chair back
  90–210f  HERO idle breathing (2px, 60f period); eyes blink at 150f
TEXT:
  [VOICE] "you're tired." at (450,190) in 90f, hold 90f, out 20f — fade+rise 12px, red glow
SFX:         clock_tick loop -18dB; low drone swell at 90f
MUSIC:       M1 continues (piano, sparse)
CAMERA:      static wide; slow push-in scale 1.00→1.04 over shot
TRANSITION:  hard cut on beat
NOTES:       shadow touches only the chair, never the hero (touch reserved for DOPAMINE-type "just one more")
```

**Field rules**
- `### S##` = sequential shot id. `B##` = master beat id (Section 2). Optional `G#` for grind sub-beats.
- `FRAMES` is absolute in/out at the project fps, with seconds and length in parentheses. Shots must tile the timeline with no gaps.
- `SETTING` uses environment asset ids plus the ritual object's displayed value, the light state and the floor line y.
- `CHARACTERS` lists one line per character: `pos` = foot anchor, `pose` and `expr` = ids from the rig library, `token` on/off, and `scale`/`glow` for the shadow (the escalation curve, R3).
- `ACTION` is frame-ranged and relative to the shot start, with an easing hint.
- `TEXT` uses `[ROLE] "string" at (x,y) in Nf, hold Nf, out Nf — animation`. ROLE ∈ {NARRATOR, VOICE, INFO, COUNTER, REP, TITLE, SLOGAN}, and each role carries its color/case per R11. Couplets are written as two TEXT lines with line 2 `in` = line 1 `in` + 36–45f.
- `SFX` lists ids + level + frame offsets.
- `MUSIC` gives the cue id + state (start / continue / hit at f / stop).
- `CAMERA` ∈ {static, push-in, pull-out, parallax-pan(speed px/s per layer), shake(amp,f), cu-cut}.
- `TRANSITION` ∈ {hard cut, fade-black Nf, match-cut (same framing), smash cut}.
- `NOTES` holds continuity/callback references (e.g., "callback V5", "bookend of S02").
