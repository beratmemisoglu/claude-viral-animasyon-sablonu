# 10 · Packaging, Titles, Thumbnails, Descriptions & Channel Strategy

Research agent 10/10. Scope: why **DISCIPLINE | An Animated Short Film** reached 3.1M views while the rest of the catalog got 29K–283K, and a packaging system the skill can reuse for new topics (title, thumbnail, description, Shorts, release plan, metrics).

> **Source honesty.** I could not find the "Day One Films" channel through web search. It is probably too new to be indexed, and searches return unrelated "Day One" films. Everything about the channel comes from `00-brief/BRIEF.md` and the 20 reference frames. Platform facts are cited below. Third-party CTR "studies" are mostly vendor data with unclear methods, so treat them as **directional only**. Items marked **[H]** are my hypotheses.

**What the data says**

1. **DISCIPLINE outperformed everything else by about 10×.** The next best video (DOPAMINE) earns ~22K/day against its ~221K/day. Lifetime views fall off fast with age (decay), so per-day rates *underestimate* older videos and the gap is real.
2. **DISCIPLINE broke out on a channel with almost no audience.** It and "How One Old Book Fixed His Life" are the oldest uploads, both 14 days old. One reached 3.1M and the other 39K. Subscribers can't explain that. The **topic + package + film** combination did it. Everything uploaded later rides DISCIPLINE's halo: viewers come from its suggested panel and from the subscribers it created.
3. **A one-word title wins only when the word is a concept with existing demand.** DISCIPLINE and DOPAMINE are words millions of people already search, watch in compilations, and use as identity ("discipline over motivation", "dopamine detox"). ROOTS, READY and ONE LAST TIME are poetic but carry no pain and no search intent. They are the weakest videos with the film framing (4.6–8.9K/day). **Rule: the single word must name a pain the viewer already has a word for.**
4. **Contrarian sentence titles are the strongest second format** (13.6K and 19.3K/day). "Motivation Will Fail You." and "You're Not Lazy. You're Too Comfortable." work as accusation and reframe, which creates curiosity and pulls in DISCIPLINE's audience. They are also explicitly in the same "discipline > motivation" universe.
5. **Story titles ("How One Old Book Fixed His Life") underperform.** That one sounds like a generic faceless-channel book summary. It doesn't name a feeling, has no protagonist the viewer identifies with, and loses the prestige "film" framing.
6. **Length correlates loosely.** Every top-4 performer runs 2:31–3:33. The 4:00+ videos (ROOTS, Overthinking, ONE LAST TIME) are mid-to-low. **[H]** At ~2:40, average percentage viewed is high and the video invites rewatching.
7. **Branding is inconsistent.** "Loneliness" and "Stop Overthinking" use title case with a trailing period, while the hits use ALL CAPS with no period. **[H]** A consistent `WORD | An Animated Short Film` makes the feed look like a series, so a viewer who liked DISCIPLINE recognizes the next one instantly.

### 1.2 The viral mechanics, factor by factor

| Factor | What DISCIPLINE did | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Topic breadth** | Snoozing, phone in bed, skipping the gym, calling yourself lazy | Nearly everyone has lived the first 40 seconds. It's universal, not niche. The "motivation/discipline" audience is one of the largest on YouTube and Shorts. |
| **Title** | `DISCIPLINE \| An Animated Short Film` | (a) One searchable ALL-CAPS keyword that reads instantly on mobile. (b) "An Animated Short Film" signals **art/prestige, not a lecture**. It promises a story, so it lowers the "I'm being preached at" defense that kills tutorial-style self-help. (c) The pipe looks like a film festival or Pixar-short label. |
| **Thumbnail [H]** | Not available to us. Most likely a hero vs. red shadow on near-black. | A single red accent on black stands out in a light or colorful feed. A figure with an antagonist creates conflict at a glance. |
| **First frame / hook** | Black screen, white handwriting: "Every day started the same." Then an alarm slap, "just 5 more minutes...", "again." | There's no logo or intro. The first line is a story opening, and within ~15s the viewer thinks "that's me". Recognition is the strongest retention hook in this genre. |
| **Antagonist** | Red-glowing shadow, the inner voice | It externalizes self-talk into a visible enemy. That gives the film a fight structure, a memorable thumbnail asset, and a recurring brand villain for every topic. |
| **Length** | 2:39 | Short enough to finish. The narrative payoff (shadow shrinks to tiny) lands near the end, so completion stays high. |
| **Rewatch loop** | Opens at 5:00 with a slapped alarm and ends at 5:00 with a calm wake-up next to a tiny shadow | The bookend is satisfying, and viewers replay the montage to catch details (DAY counters, shattering words). |
| **Shareable slogan** | "Day one, or one day." Also "Motivation gets you started. Discipline keeps you going." and "Nobody is coming to save you. So save yourself." | These are already-viral quotes. A chiasmus fits on a screenshot, in a comment, or in a story repost, and people share videos to say something about themselves. |
| **Comment bait** | "What DAY are you on?", the "lazy isn't who you are, it's what you repeat" line | Viewers post their own day counts ("Day 1 starting today"), which creates a comment ritual and return visits. |
| **Algorithm fit** | Browse and suggested next to motivation, gym, and self-improvement content | YouTube describes recommendations as *pulling* content to viewers whose habits suggest it will satisfy them, with satisfaction signals (surveys, finishing, continuing to watch) beyond raw watch time [S1][S2]. A short, finishable, emotionally resolved film scores well on those signals. The huge motivation audience gave it a pool to scale into. |
| **Craft differentiation** | "Every frame, sound and note… made from scratch." Consistent hand-drawn style | It stands apart from the 2025–26 flood of AI motivation slideshows. YouTube's July 2025 YPP update targets "inauthentic", mass-produced, templated content [S7][S8]. Visible authorship is both an audience trust signal and a monetization safeguard. |

**Bottom line:** A universal pain with a known name, packaged as a film rather than advice, with a visible enemy, a 2:40 runtime, a bookend ending, and a meme-ready slogan. The other videos each lose one or more of these. ROOTS/READY lack the known pain word, the Book video lacks the film frame and identity, and the 4-minute videos lose the length advantage.

## 3. Thumbnail system

### 3.1 Principles (adapted to this style)

1. **Near-black background, one red accent, one cream hero.** That is the brand. High contrast is a baseline expectation in breakout thumbnails, not a differentiator [S3][S6]. Here it comes free with the style.
2. **Conflict in one glance:** hero plus shadow in the same frame, with the shadow larger, closer or glowing. A single subject or face with a genuine expression is the most common pattern in breakout thumbnails, and exaggerated "shock" faces are rare (~5%) [S6]. Use clear emotion (gritted teeth, slumped sadness), not a cartoon scream.
3. **Text: 0–3 words, never a repeat of the title** [S6][S9]. The best text is the **shadow's line in red, lowercase, handwritten font** ("you can't.", "stay.", "what if…"). It's new information and it's the enemy speaking. Alternatively use no text and let the title carry the word.
4. **Mobile legibility:** the hero should be ≥30% of frame height and the shadow ≥60%. In the raw frames the hero is often only 15–25% of the height, so recompose with a closer crop or rescale. Test at 168×94 px.
5. **Safe zones:** keep everything important out of the **bottom-right** (duration badge) and the bottom ~10% (progress bar on watched videos). Drop the letterbox top bar and DAY counter, which are noise at thumbnail size.
6. **Bespoke render, not a screenshot.** Render the thumbnail as its own 1280×720 Remotion still (`<Still>`) using the same rigs. Push the shadow glow intensity ~1.5× and add a soft radial spotlight on the hero.

### 3.3 Templates per new topic

| Template | Layout (1280×720) | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 Goliath** | Shadow 70% height, right third, glow; hero 30–35% height, lower-left, facing it; red lowercase line top-left, ≤3 words | Default for "fight" topics (overthinking, fear, comfort, scrolling, procrastination) |
| **T2 Mirror** | Mirror/phone screen/window frame center-right with the shadow inside; hero left, looking at it | Self-image topics (self-worth, comparison, ego, envy) |
| **T3 Spotlight** | Hero center-low in a cone of light; huge darkness; shadow just a red-eyed outline at the edge of the light | Sad topics (loneliness, grief, heartbreak, regret) |
| **T4 Topic object** | Single oversized symbolic object in red (phone, clock, heart, scale) with the hero dwarfed or attached by a red thread | Scrolling, patience, love |

**A/B plan for every upload (Test & Compare, 3 slots):**
- A = T1 Goliath with red shadow line
- B = same image, **no text**
- C = alternate template (T2 or T3)

Winner is judged by watch-time share, not CTR [S4][S5]. Log the winning template per topic into a sheet. After ~10 tests you get a house rule ("Mirror wins on self-image topics", etc.).

**Text rules summary:** 0–3 words. Lowercase handwritten. Red = enemy voice, white = (rare) hero/narrator. Never the title word. Font size ≥ 1/8 of thumbnail height. Thin dark outline or glow for readability. No emojis, arrows, or circles, which would break the prestige "film" feel.

## 4. Description template, tags, pinned comment, end screen

### 4.1 Description (mirrors the hit exactly in structure)

```
{Hook line 1 — the film's first on-screen sentence}. {Fragment 2}. {Fragment 3}. {Fragment 4 — the inner voice's lie}.

{TITLE WORD} is a short animated film about {the war against / the weight of / the voice behind} {pain}, and the moment you {decision that flips it}.

{Lesson line A.} {Lesson line B.}

0:00 {Hook sentence}
0:{xx} The mirror            ← evocative 2–4 word names, never "Intro/Part 2"
0:{xx} Rock bottom
1:{xx} Day 1
1:{xx} The grind / The climb / The silence
1:{xx} The final fight
2:{xx} The summit / The morning after
2:{xx} {End-card slogan}

Every frame, sound and note of music in this film was made from scratch.

Subscribe to Day One Films for more short films about the fights we all have with ourselves.

#{word} #shortfilm #animation
```

**Filled example: OVERTHINKING**
> It was 2 a.m. again. The ceiling. The replay. The voice that said "what if."
> OVERTHINKING is a short animated film about the voice that turns one small moment into a thousand disasters, and the night you finally stop listening.
> You can't think your way out of a feeling. You have to walk through it.
> 0:00 It was 2 a.m. again · 0:35 The replay · 0:55 The spiral · 1:10 Day 1 · 1:30 The noise · 1:55 The final fight · 2:15 Morning · 2:30 Think less. Live more.
> (credibility line + subscribe line + hashtags)

**Rules**
- The first ~150 characters show in search and above the fold, so put the hook there, not links.
- Chapters: the first timestamp must be `0:00`, at least 3 chapters, each ≥10 s, ascending, one per line [S10]. Note: the brief shows the chapters joined with "·". On YouTube they must be **on separate lines** to register.
- **Credibility line:** keep it only if it is literally true. Remotion-built visuals are made from scratch. If voice, music or SFX are licensed or AI-generated, change it to "Every frame of this film was animated from scratch" so the claim stays honest. It is also the channel's defense against the "inauthentic/mass-produced" reading of the YPP rules [S7][S8]. Vary each film's story, not just the topic word.
- Hashtags: 3 max. The first ones appear above the title. Use `#{topicword} #shortfilm #animation`. Avoid hashtag spam.

### 4.2 Tags (low weight, mainly for misspellings and synonyms)
`{word}, {word} animation, {word} short film, animated short film, motivational short film, self improvement animation, inner voice, {synonyms e.g. "overthinking at night", "how to stop overthinking"}, day one films`

### 4.3 Pinned comment (posted by the channel within the first minute)

Rotate between three proven engagement types:
1. **Ritual / status:** "What DAY are you on? Comment it and come back in 30 days to update it." This is the DISCIPLINE mechanic, and it makes people return to the video.
2. **Inner-voice confession:** "What does YOUR shadow say to you? Write it below. Then write what you'll say back."
3. **Next-film vote:** "Next film: COMPARISON or FEAR? Most-liked reply decides." This builds the pipeline and gets replies, and the result becomes a callback in the next description ("You chose this one.").

Heart and reply to early comments in the first 2 hours. Don't put links in the pinned comment on long-form videos. Do put the long-form link in Shorts pinned comments (see §5.3).

### 4.4 End screen (last ~15–20 s)

The film's end card ("{WORD}" + slogan) lasts ~5 s. Then hold a **dark 15 s outro** over frame-19-style resolution art (hero calm, shadow tiny):
- Left: **"Best for viewer"** video element. Once the catalog has 5+ films, override with the **adjacent topic** manually (DISCIPLINE → DOPAMINE → PROCRASTINATION…).
- Right: a **playlist element** ("The Fights We Have With Ourselves").
- Subscribe element on the hero.

Keep it silent or with ambient music only, and don't add a "please subscribe" voiceover, so the film mood holds.

## 5. Release & series strategy

### 5.1 Cadence
- Observed: ~10 long-form films in ~14 days (≈5/week). That works while a hit is generating suggested traffic. Every new upload gets placed next to DISCIPLINE. But quality dropped where concepts got abstract (ROOTS/READY/ONE LAST TIME).
- **Recommendation:** **2–3 films/week while a halo is active** (a video doing >20K/day). Otherwise **1–2/week** with higher craft. Never sacrifice the hook, the antagonist, or the 2:30–3:30 length for volume. Repetitive near-identical films are the specific thing YouTube's inauthentic-content rule targets [S7][S8].
- Same weekday and time each week, timed for the audience's evening (when "late-night self-reflection" viewing peaks **[H]**). Check Studio's "When your viewers are on YouTube".

### 5.2 Topic pipeline prioritization

Score each candidate 1–5 on each axis and multiply:
1. **Search/identity demand:** is it a word people already use about themselves? Check YouTube search autocomplete, Google Trends, and view counts of the top 10 results.
2. **Universality of pain:** what share of viewers has lived the first 30 s?
3. **Antagonist clarity:** can the red shadow say a 1–3 word line that everyone has heard in their head?
4. **Audience adjacency:** overlap with the current winner's viewers. Use Studio → Audience → "Other videos your audience watched".
5. **Visual set-piece:** does it have one image as strong as the mirror or the giant shadow on the stairs?

Suggested order after DISCIPLINE/DOPAMINE: **PROCRASTINATION → OVERTHINKING → COMFORT ZONE → SCROLLING → LONELINESS (re-package) → COMPARISON → FEAR → SELF-WORTH → ANXIETY → HEARTBREAK**. Grief, love and anger come later.

Use **format B contrarian titles** for ~1 in 3 uploads. They performed 2nd-best per day, and they mix "film" and "essay" packaging, which tests a second audience segment.

Recurring universe: the **same hero and same red shadow** across films. Over time the shadow becomes a character people recognize in thumbnails ("the red guy is back"). That is a compounding CTR asset **[H]**.

### 5.3 Shorts cut-downs (9:16, 1080×1920)

Render in Remotion as a separate composition, not a crop. Reflow the layout: hero lower-third, text upper-third, shadow scaled to fill the height. Each Short is 15–35 s, loops cleanly, and ends on a frame that asks for the film.

| Short | Source beat (DISCIPLINE timing) | Hook text (first 1 s) | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 "The mirror" | ~0:35–0:55, mirror shows shadow, "He believed it." | "the enemy was never outside." | Cut on the reflection's red eyes, then "full film ↓" |
| S2 "Punching words" | ~1:30–1:49, "you're lazy" shatters on every punch | "every punch was a word he heard." | Last letter shatters into the loop start |
| S3 "you can't." | ~1:49–2:04, giant shadow on the stairs, he splits it | "it got bigger the closer he got." | Hero breaks through, then summit flash |
| S4 "5:00" | 0:00–0:20 + 2:25–2:39, slapped alarm vs. calm wake-up | "same alarm. different man." | End card "Day one, or one day." |
| S5 "told you." | DAY 12 push-up, counter "3" | "the voice said 'told you.'" | Counter ticks, loop |

- Post **2–4 Shorts within 3 days of each long-form release**. Tightly aligned Shorts posted near the long-form launch reportedly drive the most long-form views [S11].
- Link both ways: use the Short's **Related video** link to the film, and put the film link in the Short's pinned comment. The pinned link is reported to convert better than the related-video chip [S11]. Treat these as directional numbers.
- Shorts mainly reach **non-subscribers** (~75% reported) [S11]. Their job is discovery and subscriber growth, not watch time. Metricool's 2026 data shows a Shorts cadence correlating with higher long-form views per video [S12] (correlation, not proof).
- Shorts title = shadow line + topic: `"you can't." #discipline #shortfilm`.

### 5.4 What to measure

| Metric | Where | Target / reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Impressions CTR, first 24–48 h** | Studio → Reach | Compare to the channel median, not to generic benchmarks. A CTR well above median with browse/suggested growing means the package works. If CTR is high but AVD is low, the thumbnail over-promises. |
| **Retention at 0:30** | Studio → Engagement → key moments | **≥70% still watching.** This tests the hook (black-screen line + "that's me" moment). A cliff before 0:15 means rewriting the hook line. |
| **Average % viewed** | Engagement | ≥60% for a 2:30–3:30 film. Spikes above 100% on the retention curve mark rewatched beats, which are your Shorts candidates. |
| **Test & Compare result** | Video details → test report | Log the winning template/text per topic [S4]. |
| **Traffic source mix** | Reach | A breakout looks like *Browse + Suggested* overtaking subscribers/notifications. Note which videos suggest yours. |
| **Engaged views → subs per 1K views** | Audience | Shows whether the film converts to the channel mission. Compare films to find which topics build the base. |
| **Returning viewers %** | Audience | Measures series health. It should rise as the shadow universe grows. |
| **End-screen element CTR** | Engagement | Tells you whether the adjacency chain (DISCIPLINE → DOPAMINE → …) is working. |
| **Comments per 1K views + "DAY X" comments** | Comments | The ritual signal. Pinned-comment variants can be compared with this. |
| **Shorts: viewed vs. swiped %, related-video clicks** | Shorts analytics | ≥70% viewed vs. swiped means the first-second hook works. Clicks show how well it feeds long-form. |

**Post-mortem rule:** for every upload, after 7 days record views/day, CTR, AVD%, 0:30 retention, title format, thumbnail template, and pain-word demand score. The skill can then learn which title format and template to output per topic.
